Seventeen years ago, Darwin Malevolent lived as a noble heir to a quiet but influential house—one tied to one of the most dangerous secrets in the realm. His family were not human. They were metal dragons, ancient beings who had long since abandoned open dominion in favor of concealment. 

Under human guises, they integrated into noble society, carefully masking their nature as “refined technique” rather than sorcery. 

Their true craft, however, was far more valuable—and far more dangerous. They were master producers of rare and superior metal materials, supplying entire nations with weapons, armor, and infrastructure-grade resources forged through draconic methods disguised as craftsmanship. It was not conquest that sustained them, but commerce and precision. For generations, it worked. 

Until it didn’t.

A monarch—driven by fear, greed, or the desire to control what could no longer be bargained with—ordered the seizure of their operation. What followed was not a negotiation, but an execution. The royal forces came prepared for compliance. They found resistance instead. Darwin’s family fought back, but they did not fight like conquerors. Metal dragons are creators by nature—measured, restrained, unwilling to destroy what could still be repurposed. That restraint became their downfall. 

The rebellion was crushed, and House Malevolent was burned from the records of nobility. Darwin survived. A child among ruins, the last living heir of a fallen noble bloodline, he was left with nothing but ash, silence, and a single relic from his past—a pendant he never lets go of, though he refuses to speak of its meaning. From that day forward, Darwin Malevolent became something else entirely. 

He did not seek immediate revenge. He did not charge toward the throne in blind fury. Instead, he learned patience—the kind that outlives kingdoms. Now, as a wandering engineer and mechanic, he is known only as a man in a strange suit of self-built metal armor, powered by intricate mechanisms that blur the line between engineering and something far more unnatural. He travels from place to place offering solutions, repairs, and inventions that defy common understanding, all while maintaining a firm insistence that what he creates is not magic—only design. 

But beneath the surface, everything he does serves a single, long-term objective. Revenge. Not through war. Not through armies. Not through chaos. Darwin Malevolent is building something far more precise. 

An invention. A system. An inevitability that will one day reach the throne that destroyed his bloodline and return everything in the only language he respects: controlled, irreversible consequence. And when that moment comes, it will not be rage that drives him forward. It will be completion.
